Booster Separation Mechanism

  • Team lead, 5 people
  • Concept downselect
  • Test jig & pin study
  • SolidWorks, PLA, aluminum

As Center Stage Team Lead I'm running research and concept development for the mechanism that releases both side boosters at apogee. The team evaluated an explosive pin concept, explosive-bolt testing, and an electromagnetic release before downselecting to an e-match-ignited pin ejection system.

Right now I'm overseeing 3D-printed test models and a jig-and-pin setup we use to measure separation angle and the force required to separate cleanly, so we know the numbers before committing to flight hardware. The mechanism was modeled and prototyped in SolidWorks and PLA, and the flight parts are being machined from aluminum on a manual lathe.

CNC Hot-Wire Airfoil

  • SolidWorks surfaces
  • DevFoam · Mach3
  • G-code, 4-axis hot wire

I modeled the airfoil's aerodynamic surface in SolidWorks, then converted the 3D geometry into 2D toolpaths for the CNC hot-wire cutter. In DevFoam and Mach3 I generated and checked the G-code, dialing in feed rate, wire temperature, and axis alignment so the finished foam held the intended profile instead of a melted approximation of it.

Canards & Vertical Stabilizer

  • Airfoil profile design
  • 4.5-ft manual hot-wire rig
  • CNC-milled canards

I designed and built the booster's canards, shaping custom airfoil profiles around the manufacturing constraints we actually had. To cut the larger tapered surfaces I built a 4.5-ft manual hot-wire rig with a rigid, aligned frame, accounting for heat transfer through the wire so the cuts came out clean without burning through or weakening the foam.

Booster Assembly & Flight

  • Fiberglass & epoxy layups
  • Bondo, sanding, paint
  • Motor, wiring, actuators
  • Launch prep & post-flight review

I helped manufacture and assemble the booster end to end: cutting foam components, integrating the canards, vertical stabilizer, fuselage sections, and nose cone, and laying up multiple fiberglass coats over the structure. From there it was finishing work — Bondo, sanding, paint — then motor assembly, wiring, actuator troubleshooting, launch prep, and reviewing what happened after each flight.

Fig. 1.5

Servo Housing

  • SolidWorks
  • FDM printing, 3+ iterations
  • Flap actuation alignment

I designed a servo housing in SolidWorks to align the servo motors precisely with the airfoil flaps, iterating through three-plus printed versions to get the fitment and electronic alignment right. Mounted inside the fuselage at the booster's center, it's what let the flaps actuate correctly in flight.
